Implant traceability.

Recall which implants and materials you used, as recorded in your own cases, with a link back to the case you logged them in.

Get Haarks Clinic

The problem

Implant choices and materials matter for planning, for revisions, and for continuity of care, but they are buried in individual operative records. Answering "what did I use, and when" usually means pulling notes one by one.

How Haarks approaches it

When you log a case, the implant and material details you record become part of your private, queryable layer. You can then ask across your own history and Haarks retrieves the matching cases, showing the records the answer is drawn from.

What the surgeon asks

  • "What implants did I previously use for revision hip arthroplasty?"
  • "Which mesh did I use in my last open hernia repairs?"
  • "Show me my previous cases using a specific implant system."

What can be retrieved

  • The implants, materials, and sizes you recorded in your cases.
  • The cases where you used them, with dates and your own notes.
  • Links to the source records so you can verify the detail.

Limitations

Implant traceability reflects only what you documented in your own cases. It is a personal record aid, not a regulated implant registry, recall system, or medical device, and it does not make clinical decisions. All clinical decisions remain the responsibility of the treating surgeon.

Who it is for

Surgeons who implant devices or use materials they need to recall later, particularly in arthroplasty, trauma, and reconstructive work.
