How It Works

Designed Around People. Not Shared Logins.

The hospital engagement model is built around individual membership and organizational separation, not a single shared account that everyone uses.

01
Hospital

A hospital environment is established.

The hospital has its own dedicated organizational environment within Haarks, separate from any individual surgeon's workspace and scoped to the institution.

02
Knowledge

Relevant hospital knowledge is connected and organized.

Protocols, SOPs, clinical guidelines, and institutional documents are connected to the hospital's environment, organized into an intelligence layer that authorized users can work with.

03
Surgeons

Authorized surgeons join individually.

Each surgeon joins the hospital's Haarks environment through their own individual membership. Their personal workspace remains their own, distinct from the hospital organization.

04
Intelligence

Authorized users access the hospital knowledge layer alongside their private workspace.

Within appropriate authorization, surgeons can interact with both the hospital's organizational intelligence and their own private knowledge, without either crossing into the other.

05
Continuity

Hospital-owned knowledge remains with the hospital as membership changes.

When a surgeon's membership ends, the hospital's knowledge stays with the hospital. What was the surgeon's stays with the surgeon. The architecture is designed so that neither party loses what is theirs.

The Hospital Opportunity

ROI, Security, and Deployment.

Three questions every hospital asks. Here is an honest view of where the value could come from, how the data is protected, and how an engagement would actually work, with a clear line between what exists today and what is a pilot or plan.

A · Return on investment

Value from knowledge that already exists.

Hospitals already own years of protocols, case records, and surgeon expertise. The opportunity is to make that knowledge usable. Potential value areas include:

  • • Institutional knowledge retention as surgeons join, move, or retire
  • • Better visibility of follow-up and documented outcomes
  • • Faster access to surgeon-specific information and protocols
  • • Reduced time spent searching across scattered drives and documents
  • • Surgical procurement and equipment opportunities via connected tools

Any financial figures discussed in a pilot are illustrative scenarios based on a hospital's own data, not guaranteed savings. Haarks does not promise a fixed return, and outcomes depend on how a hospital uses the platform.

B · Security & privacy

Private by architecture, not by promise.

  • • Ownership & isolation. Each surgeon's workspace is private and isolated; hospital knowledge is kept in organizational scope. The two are not merged, and knowledge is not used to train shared models.
  • • Connected storage. Source files you connect stay in your own storage; Haarks indexes derived data, extracted text, metadata, and embeddings, into a private, account-scoped layer rather than copying raw files into permanent storage.
  • • Access control. Access is organized around membership and authorization, scoped per environment.
  • • AI processing. Queries are processed by third-party AI providers acting as sub-processors, only to generate your answer. See the Privacy Policy.
  • • Encryption. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

Haarks is designed with privacy and applicable data-protection requirements in mind. We do not claim HIPAA, GDPR, DPDP, or SOC 2 certification; specific compliance and deployment requirements are scoped per hospital engagement.

C · Deployment

How a hospital would work with Haarks.

  • • Pilot first. Engagements begin as a pilot with a small surgical team, scoped to the hospital's structure.
  • • Surgeon onboarding. Surgeons keep their own private workspace; the hospital connects institutional knowledge in organizational scope.
  • • Permissions. Access to hospital knowledge follows membership and authorization.
  • • When a surgeon leaves. Private surgeon knowledge stays with the surgeon; hospital-owned knowledge stays with the hospital. Ownership and migration are agreed up front.
  • • Hospital administration. Organization-level administration is part of the hospital direction and is scoped per pilot, not a finished, commercially deployed product today.
